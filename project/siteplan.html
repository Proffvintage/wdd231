UCHIHA CAR CARE

Website Site Plan

Site Name

UCHIHA CAR CARE

The name pairs a distinctive family name with a plain description of the work, so it reads as a small, owner-operated business rather than a franchise. “Car Care” keeps the service obvious at a glance for people searching locally, while “Uchiha” is short, memorable, and easy to say aloud — useful for word-of-mouth referrals, which are the main way detailing shops grow.

Representative domain: uchihacarcare.com

Site Purpose

UCHIHA CAR CARE is a car detailing service website built to give a small, growing business a credible online presence and a way to turn visitors into booked jobs. The site presents a before-and-after gallery of completed details, introduces the owner and the company’s approach, and publishes transparent pricing for its service tiers — interior detail, exterior detail, full detail, and engine bay cleaning. A quote calculator lets visitors estimate the cost of a job by vehicle size and service level before they commit, and a tips-and-tricks blog shares practical car care advice so the site stays useful to owners between appointments. A contact form handles booking requests and questions.

Color Scheme

Four colors carry the whole site. This planning document is styled with these colors exclusively, so the page doubles as a live sample of the palette.

Palette and where each color is applied
Swatch Hex Name Where it is used
#0D1321 Rich Black Header and footer backgrounds, nav bar, all body and heading text
#6197D9 Blue Gray Primary accent: button backgrounds, link underlines, section rules, icons
#B0CBEC Powder Blue Secondary accent: card and table-row backgrounds, hover states, pull quotes
#FFFFFF White Page background, content surfaces, text on the dark header and footer

Contrast check: Rich Black on White is 18.5:1 and Rich Black on Powder Blue is 11.1:1, both well past the WCAG AA minimum of 4.5:1. Blue Gray is reserved for backgrounds, rules, and underlines rather than small text, because Blue Gray on White measures only 3.0:1.

Typography

Two typefaces, both served from Google Fonts.

Alan Sans
Used for all headings, the logo wordmark, the navigation bar, and button labels. Its wider letterforms and heavier available weights give headings enough presence to anchor a page without needing a display face.
Roboto
Used for body copy, lists, table content, form labels, captions, and footer text. It stays readable at small sizes on phones, which is where most visitors will read pricing and blog posts.

A generic sans-serif is declared last in every font stack as a fallback. It is a fallback, not a third typeface choice.
